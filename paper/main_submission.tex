Toward a Telemetry Layer for AgenticOS}
\author{Anonymous Author(s)}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Agentic AI systems are evaluated today through three disjoint public infrastructures---leaderboards, trace corpora, and telemetry logs---none of the public sources we examine jointly exposes the fields a resource-aware operating layer would need: task identity, model identity, scaffold and policy-configuration identity, outcome, token/cache usage, retries, latency, and billing. We measure its consequences across three studies on public data. First, using the Holistic Agent Leaderboard (\hal{}), raw dollar-cost rankings transfer across some benchmark pairs (Spearman $\rho{=}0.74$--$0.93$) but are mostly absorbed by a workload-persistent displayed-label cost propensity (pooled model $R^2{=}82.0\%$); success-adjusted cost is markedly less portable (mean $\rho{=}0.59$ vs.\ $0.84$). Second, using 977 exact task--model--matched trajectory pairs from Open-SWE-Traces, scaffold choice changes observable execution burden at fixed task and model (OpenHands/SWE-agent turn ratio $0.85$, $[0.82,0.87]$), and the widely assumed ``expensive failure'' premium is not detected under exact task matching ($80\%$-power threshold: premium fractions $\geq 0.65$; observed $0.44$--$0.49$). Third, auditing two independent telemetry corpora ($8{,}058$ and $1{,}017$ sessions), we characterize the resource anatomy---$93.7\%$ mean cache-read share, $4.29\times$ conditional tool-error cascades replicated across sources, top-$1\%$-of-sessions holding $48.3\%$ of input-token volume---that a scheduler or router would need but that no benchmark currently attaches to an outcome. These studies are complementary rather than linked: Studies~1--2 show that transferred raw-cost rankings and unconditioned failure premiums confound \emph{which workloads were attempted} with \emph{how agents behaved on them}, while Study~3 shows that the execution variables hidden beneath those aggregates are themselves heterogeneous, heavy-tailed, and state-dependent. From this evidence we derive a joint telemetry schema spanning identity, execution, resources, control, and outcome, and argue it belongs at the operating-system layer: controlling execution and allocating resources requires observing what those allocations depend on.
\end{abstract}

\section{Why This Belongs at the OS Layer}
\label{sec:os-layer}
%% ============================================================

Agentic AI systems persist state, invoke tools, retry failed actions, and accumulate context across long horizons. Reasoning about their resource behavior---for scheduling, routing, cost budgeting, or governance---requires the same unified telemetry that operating systems have long provided for conventional workloads: a common schema linking \emph{what ran}, \emph{on what}, \emph{under which policy}, \emph{with what outcome}, and \emph{at what resource cost}. We use \emph{observability debt} to name the accumulated inability of public agent-evaluation infrastructure to jointly attribute resource consumption to task, model, execution policy, outcome, and system-level events. In every public source we examined, this debt prevents reliable attribution of observed resource differences to workload, model, scaffold, configuration, or execution behavior.

What makes this an \emph{operating-system} responsibility rather than better benchmark bookkeeping? An agentic OS occupies a specific functional position. It observes \emph{identity} (which task, model, scaffold, and policy configuration is running), \emph{execution} (which tools act, what context accumulates), \emph{resources} (tokens, cache, time, memory, money), \emph{control} (retry, terminate, route, budget), and \emph{outcome} (did the task succeed). The same resource-accounting principle motivates an OS-level treatment: the layer that controls execution and allocates resources needs access to the telemetry required to make those decisions. Without per-round token components, a memory manager cannot budget context growth; without configuration identity, a router cannot learn \emph{why} one label is cheap; without error-status streams, a governor cannot halt a spiraling session. Today, each of these decisions is made implicitly from aggregate dollar figures (Figure~\ref{fig:loop}).

We treat three public data sources as stand-ins for what this observability layer would unify: a cost-and-outcome leaderboard (\hal{}), a task/model/scaffold-matched trajectory corpus (Open-SWE-Traces), and raw session-level usage telemetry (two independent corpora). Each exposes a different partial view. \hal{} links dollar cost to outcome but encrypts execution traces. Open-SWE-Traces exposes exact task/model/scaffold matching and resolved status but no tokens or billing. The telemetry corpora expose token, cache, and tool-call dynamics but no outcome or billing. \textbf{No public source we examined jointly exposes task, model, scaffold and policy configuration, outcome, token/cache usage, retries, latency, and billing}, the joint schema a resource-aware OS layer needs (Table~\ref{tab:gap}). Importantly, combining these sources does not create record-level observability: their different execution units, identifiers, and release boundaries prevent resource use, outcome, and billing from being joined into a single per-execution record.

Our three studies are therefore complementary rather than linked---each probes a different dimension of the gap, and none can be joined to another at the record level. (1)~A coverage-aware cost-portability study (\hal{}) shows that aggregate dollar cost conflates displayed-label propensity with benchmark difficulty and decorrelates under success adjustment. (2)~A matched-trajectory study shows that execution structure differs measurably across scaffolds, while a popular failure-cost heuristic turns out to measure workload composition. (3)~A cross-source telemetry audit shows that the execution variables hidden beneath aggregate cost are themselves highly heterogeneous, heavy-tailed, and state-dependent. Together they triangulate one gap; no pair of them jointly identifies a causal mechanism. This paper is an empirical requirements study for an AgenticOS telemetry layer, rather than an implementation of the layer itself.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}
%% ============================================================

Kapoor et al.\ (2026) introduce \hal{} as cost-aware evaluation infrastructure spanning coding, web, science, and customer-service tasks (21,730 rollouts, roughly \$40,000 in evaluation cost). Kapoor et al.\ (2024) motivate joint accuracy--cost reporting and convex-hull frontier analysis, which we adopt as one of three decision-frontier definitions. Ndzomga (2026) asks whether task subsets preserve agent rankings within a single benchmark; we instead ask whether an \emph{observed cost signal} predicts a \emph{different} workload. Benchmark$^2$ studies cross-benchmark rank consistency for static LLM benchmarks; our setting adds interactive scaffolds, dollar cost, success adjustment, and a held-out workload test. SWE-Effi reports that failed attempts consume substantially more resources than successful ones; our matched design tests that claim at fixed task and does not detect the premium once task-difficulty confounding is removed (Section~\ref{sec:matched}). Open-SWE-Traces supplies the exact matching that makes this test possible but lacks token/billing fields, motivating Section~\ref{sec:schema}. Commercial tracers expose per-deployment spans but are not cross-benchmark evaluation, and public releases strip execution detail, so the per-request visibility our audited sources lack is exactly what we quantify.

\section{Study 1: Does a Cost Signal Travel Across Workloads?}
\label{sec:cost}
%% ============================================================

\subsection{Data and cohort}
We use the public HAL CSV snapshot (Ndzomga, 2026): 242 displayed evaluations across nine benchmarks, 11 scaffolds, 29 labels, supplying accuracy and aggregate dollar cost but no token components, retries, or per-task trajectories---the gap this paper is about.

The only broadly repeated scaffold is the displayed \hal{} Generalist Agent; fixing it and matching exact label strings yields six adequately overlapping workloads (CORE-Bench Hard, GAIA, SciCode, ScienceAgentBench, SWE-bench Verified Mini, TAU-bench Airline), a cohort of 86 rows and 25 labels.

\subsection{Raw cost transfers; the mechanism behind it does not}
Across six pairs with ${\geq}12$ shared labels, raw dollar-cost Spearman $\rho$ ranges $0.74$--$0.93$ (mean $0.84$), surviving Holm-corrected permutation testing (Figure~\ref{fig:rank}). A pooled label-plus-benchmark log-cost fixed-effects model explains $R^2{=}82.0\%$ (adjusted $72.7\%$); once label and benchmark propensity are removed, \textbf{no high-overlap residual-cost association survives} a 20,000-draw permutation test.

Leave-one-benchmark-out (LOBO) prediction succeeds for CORE ($\rho{=}0.78$, $N{=}14$, $p{=}0.0016$), GAIA ($0.87$, $N{=}16$), SWE-mini ($0.90$, $N{=}16$), TAU ($0.83$, $N{=}14$). It fails for SciCode (unstable, $[-0.50,0.14]$) and ScienceAgentBench (directionally negative, $[-0.77,-0.43]$).

\textbf{Interpretation:} the portable component behaves as a workload-persistent displayed-label propensity---plausibly reflecting provider pricing, reasoning-mode defaults, or routing policy---that a router could in principle learn and reuse. but public data cannot identify \emph{which}, since the distinguishing fields are not exposed.

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.48\linewidth]{figures/rank_transfer_compact.pdf}
\caption{\textbf{Raw cost transfers; success-adjusted cost does not.} Pairwise Spearman $\rho$ for benchmark pairs sharing ${\geq}12$ labels. Raw dollar cost is portable (mean $0.84$); cost per expected success is not (mean $0.59$), only three CPS pairs survive correction.}
\label{fig:rank}
\end{figure}

\subsection{Success-adjusted cost is a different, less portable ranking}
Cost per expected success, $\mathrm{CPS}_{i,b}(\epsilon)=C_{i,b}/\max(A_{i,b},\epsilon)$ with primary floor $\epsilon{=}0.01$ (sensitivities at $0.001$, $0.05$, and zero-exclusion in the supplement), has mean $\rho{=}0.59$. Only three pairs survive correction: CORE--GAIA ($0.87$, $[0.63,0.96]$), CORE--TAU ($0.83$, $[0.31,1.00]$), GAIA--TAU ($0.78$, $[0.42,0.94]$). The clearest raw-vs-CPS gap is GAIA--SWE-mini ($0.60$, $[0.14,1.13]$): a label cheap in dollars is not reliably cheap per expected success elsewhere. No label tested on ${\geq}5$ workloads sits on every accuracy--cost frontier under discrete nondominance, convex hull, or $5\%$ tolerance (o4-mini Low reaches $5/6$ under tolerance; no universal winner under any strict definition).

\textbf{Implication:} Routing on displayed dollar cost therefore optimizes a signal whose portability degrades substantially once success is taken into account.

\section{Study 2: What Matched Trajectories Reveal That Aggregates Cannot}
\label{sec:matched}
%% ============================================================

\hal{}'s own trace archives are encrypted, so Study~1 cannot say \emph{why} costs differ. We use an external unencrypted corpus---977 exact task--model pairs from Open-SWE-Traces for MiniMax-M2.5 under two scaffolds (OpenHands, SWE-agent), a reproducible but nonrandom four-shard boundary sample. The schema exposes resolved status, roles, tool calls, and text but not billed tokens or dollars; we treat turns, tool calls, and characters as trace-burden proxies.

\subsection{Scaffold choice changes observable burden at fixed task}
OpenHands has lower matched medians: 123 vs.\ 149 trajectory turns (ratio $0.85$, $[0.82,0.87]$), 61 vs.\ 74 tool calls ($0.85$, $[0.82,0.86]$), 176{,}708 vs.\ 194{,}263 characters ($0.95$, $[0.92,0.98]$). Resolved status shows no detected difference ($p{=}0.197$): scaffolds differ in execution structure without a detected accuracy trade-off here.

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.49\linewidth]{figures/matched_trace_burden.pdf}\hfill
\includegraphics[width=0.49\linewidth]{figures/failure_burden_discordant.pdf}
\caption{\textbf{(a)} Scaffold burden on 977 matched pairs: OpenHands uses $\sim$$15\%$ fewer median turns/tool calls than SWE-agent at fixed task and model. \textbf{(b)} The failure premium is not detected under exact task matching ($80\%$ power against ${\geq}15$pp). Left: naive cross-task ratios reproduce folklore ($19$--$36\%$ heavier failures). Right: among 107 discordant pairs, failed side heavier only $47$--$52/107$.}
\label{fig:burden}
\end{figure}

\subsection{The ``expensive failure'' premium is not detected under exact task matching}
Comparing failed vs.\ resolved trajectories \emph{across} tasks reproduces SWE-Effi's pattern ($19$--$36\%$ heavier failures). But among the \textbf{107 discordant pairs}---identical task and model, one scaffold resolved and the other failed---the failed side is heavier only 47--52 of 107 on every metric (binomial $p{\geq}0.25$). An $80\%$-power pre-specified test against premiums ${\geq}15$pp finds the observed $0.44$--$0.49$ uninformative for smaller effects; one model and a nonrandom sample bound the claim.

\textbf{Interpretation:} hard tasks make agents both burn more \emph{and} fail more. An unconditioned failure multiplier measures \emph{which tasks were attempted}, not a property of failing runs. Task-conditioned accounting requires exactly the joint schema no single public source provides.

\section{Study 3: The Telemetry an OS Layer Would Need to See Directly}
\label{sec:telemetry}
%% ============================================================

Studies 1--2 show aggregate cost hides \emph{why} an agent is expensive and that execution structure varies across systems at fixed task and model. This section audits raw execution telemetry across two independent coding-agent corpora (Table~\ref{tab:anatomy}). \textbf{TraceLab}: $8{,}058$ sessions, $665{,}453$ rounds, $743{,}819$ tool calls. \textbf{MIMO Claude Code}: $1{,}017$ sessions, $4{,}690$ rounds. Neither links usage to outcome or billing.

\textbf{Context is the dominant resource, not generation.} The median TraceLab round carries 132{,}092 input tokens against 249 output tokens ($93.7\%$ mean cache-read share); median final-round context is $3.6\times$ initial (P90 $9.9\times$). A dollar total cannot separate price- from cache-policy- or accumulation-driven spend. Tool verbosity drives it: median next-input growth is 443 / 1{,}412 / 5{,}420 tokens below / within / above 10k tool-result characters.

\textbf{Resource burden is heavy-tailed.} The top $1\%$ of sessions hold $48.3\%$ of summed input volume; top $10\%$ hold $88.2\%$. Median session input rises from 0.34M tokens at zero observed tool errors to 14.65M at six or more; mean-based budgeting would miss this tail-driven concentration (Figure~\ref{fig:tail}).

\textbf{Error cascades replicate across independent sources.} After a successful call the next errs $4.9\%$, after an error $21.0\%$ ($4.29\times$); MIMO replicates at $4.33\times$ ($4.3\%$ vs.\ $1.0\%$). Same-tool retry is common ($88.3\%$ get one, $77.9\%$ succeed) but the tail is extreme (consecutive-error runs: median 1, P90 3, max 368). Failures are also slower yet less informative: 313 ms vs.\ 140 ms (P95 85.6 s vs.\ 15.8 s), returning 208 vs.\ 602 characters.

\textbf{Data-quality boundary.} MIMO repeats one constant per-round usage tuple, so its within-session token dynamics are not validated; we use it only for session counts, totals, concentration, and content-derived error sequences.

\section{An Evidence-Derived Joint Telemetry Schema}
\label{sec:schema}
%% ============================================================

Table~\ref{tab:gap} shows the coverage gap; the missing rows are exactly those Sections~\ref{sec:cost}--\ref{sec:telemetry} show to be load-bearing. Each field is tied to an analysis above or to the OS control function it unblocks (\emph{evidence-derived}: usefulness and necessity for those functions, not formal minimality). Keyed to that function:

\begin{enumerate}[nosep,leftmargin=1.5em]
\item \emph{(Attribution / exact matching)} \textbf{Task and model identity} (exact IDs, not display strings)---enables exact-matched designs; without it the Section~\ref{sec:matched} test is infeasible on public data.
\item \emph{(Routing between scaffolds)} \textbf{Scaffold/harness identity and version}---attributes burden differences to scaffold design rather than model behavior; input to routing.
\item \emph{(Configuration attribution)} \textbf{Configuration and policy identity} (reasoning mode, sampling params, budgets, routing/cache policy)---distinct from model identity; Study~1's unexplained propensity component is plausibly the shadow of these missing fields.
\item \emph{(Success-conditioned accounting)} \textbf{Outcome/resolved status}---conditions any cost statistic on success, closing the CPS portability gap.
\item \emph{(Memory / budget management)} \textbf{Token components per round (input, output, cache-read, cache-creation)}---separates price from accumulation and cache policy; lets a memory layer budget context growth.
\item \emph{(Governance / halting)} \textbf{Tool-call counts, retries, error status per call}---detects and prices $4.29\times$ cascade dynamics; a design motivation for governance policies that halt spiraling sessions---motivated here, not experimentally validated.
\item \emph{(Real-time scheduling)} \textbf{Latency per call and per session}---present only as unlinked tool-call durations in one corpus; required for real-time scheduling.
\item \emph{(Procurement / auditing)} \textbf{Billing, linked to all of the above}---the one field \hal{} exposes well, and exactly the field that is uninterpretable without the others.
\end{enumerate}

\noindent Figure~\ref{fig:loop} places these fields at the stages of the control loop they close; Table~\ref{tab:gap} shows why, on public data alone, the loop does not close.

\section{Limits}
\label{sec:limits}
%% ============================================================

This is a secondary-data analysis with clear boundaries. Display labels do not fully identify prompts, tools, budgets, or harness versions; the \hal{} cohort is sparse, unbalanced, mostly single-run. Propensity adjustment is observational, not list-price control; CPS is an aggregate ratio, not mean cost of successful runs. Study~2 uses one model and a nonrandom sample whose proxies are not tokens or dollars, powered only against strong premiums (${\geq}15$pp). Study~3 is observational and unlinked to outcomes or billing; MIMO's per-round fields are unverifiable and used only where supported, and its design implications are motivations, not evaluated policies. Each limit is itself an instance of the missing-field problem.

%% ============================================================
\section{Conclusion}
%% ============================================================

Across three studies, aggregate resource signals cannot be reliably attributed to workload, execution, or outcome without telemetry fields the public sources we examined do not jointly expose. Raw-cost rankings transfer across workloads but are strongly associated with a workload-persistent displayed-label cost propensity; success-adjusted cost reorders the ranking (three of six pairs survive correction); the assumed failure-cost premium is not detected under exact task matching. No examined source jointly links task, model, scaffold and configuration, outcome, token/cache usage, retries, latency, and billing. The layer that controls execution and allocates resources is the natural owner of this telemetry, so we argue the schema belongs at a shared operating-system layer for agentic AI rather than being reconstructed piecemeal inside each leaderboard or trace release.
